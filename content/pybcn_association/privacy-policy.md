PyBCN is a volunteer association. This page says what personal data this website involves, why we have it, who else sees it, and how to ask us to change or remove it. We wrote it to be read, so it is short. If something is not covered here, ask us.

## Who is responsible

**Associació Python Barcelona** (CIF G67254045) is the data controller.

- **Email**: [pybcn@googlegroups.com](mailto:pybcn@googlegroups.com)
- **Registered address**: Carrer de Concepció Arenal, 165, 08027 Barcelona

The Board handles requests about personal data. We have no data protection officer: the law does not require one for an association of our size and activity.

## What data this site involves, and why

### People pages

The site has pages for the people who organise our events, speak at them, mentor at workshops, or collaborate with the association. Each page shows a name, a photograph, a short biography, the role in PyBCN or at an event, and links to the person's own profiles (GitHub, LinkedIn, Twitter, personal site).

- **Where it comes from**: for most people, the person. They sent the photograph and wrote or approved the text for publication here. For speakers that the archive credits, the public announcements of our own Meetup events: the page then shows the name, the talks, and the events. A page with no photograph shows the initials of the name on a colour instead; the grid of organizers or speakers on an event page shows a silhouette.
- **Why we have it**: for a page that the person sent, their consent (GDPR article 6.1.a). You can withdraw it at any time, and we remove the page. Withdrawing consent does not affect what was published before. For a page built from our announcements, our legitimate interest in keeping the history of the community (GDPR article 6.1.f). You can object to it, and we remove the page.
- **How long we keep it**: until the person asks us to remove it, or until we take the page down ourselves because it is out of date.
- **One thing to know**: the source of this site is a public Git repository on GitHub. When we remove a page, it disappears from the site and from the current version of the repository. The old text remains in the repository history, as with any Git project. Tell us if you also want it gone from there and we will work it out with you. Copies kept by search engines and web archives are outside our control.

### Event photographs

Some event pages show photographs from the posts of the association on X, with the caption they were posted with. Only photographs that the association published itself are on the site. They show the event in general: a speaker gave the talk in public and accepted that it was recorded, and the audience appears in the background and from a distance.

- **Where it comes from**: the public posts of the association on X.
- **Why we have it**: our legitimate interest in keeping the history of the community (GDPR article 6.1.f). You can object to a photograph in which you appear, and we remove it.
- **How long we keep it**: until someone objects to it, or until we remove the gallery.

### Forms

A few pages link to or embed a Google Form:

- the **membership form**, to become a member of the association;
- the **propose a talk** form, for a talk at a PyBCN meetup;
- the **PyLadies BCN call for proposals** form;
- the **call for proposals** of each PyDay BCN edition (workshops and lightning talks), and the speaker, mentor, and attendee forms of some workshops.

Each form asks for what it needs to handle your request: usually your name, an email address to answer you, and the content of your proposal or your membership details.

- **Where it goes**: into a Google Forms spreadsheet in a Google account run by the association's organizers. Google stores it under its own [privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).
- **Why we have it**: to do what you asked for, which is the membership agreement or the steps you take to propose a talk or join a workshop (GDPR article 6.1.b).
- **Who sees it**: the organizers of that event or working group.
- **How long we keep it**: responses to an event form are needed until the event is over and its programme is published. We have no automatic deletion after that. Ask us and we delete your response. Membership data stays in the member register for as long as you are a member; the law requires associations to keep that register and the related accounting records for some years after.

### Membership payments

The membership fee is paid on [PayPal](https://www.paypal.com)'s site through a Subscribe button. PayPal tells us your name, your email address, and the amount paid. We never see your card or bank details. PayPal is a separate controller under its own [privacy statement](https://www.paypal.com/es/legalhub/privacy-full). We keep the payment record because the accounting and tax rules require it.

### Email

Our contact addresses, such as pybcn@googlegroups.com and pyladies-bcn@googlegroups.com, are Google Groups. A message you send there is stored in the group and read by the organizers who belong to that group. We use it to answer you and to follow up on what you asked (GDPR article 6.1.b and 6.1.f). The thread stays in the group archive; ask us and we delete it.

### Embedded Google content

A few pages embed a Google Form or a Google Calendar in a frame: the two talk proposal forms and the agenda of PyDay BCN 2019. The frame is served by Google: when the page loads, Google sees your IP address and browser details, and may set its own cookies inside the frame. Google's [privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) applies to that.

Some pages embed a video in a frame from **youtube-nocookie.com**, the YouTube player Google serves for sites that do not want it to set cookies. Four kinds of page carry one: the page of a talk that was recorded, one frame per talk; the page of a meetup whose session was recorded as a whole, one frame per recording; the page of a person, one frame per recorded talk they gave; and the home page, which plays the presentation of the Barcelona community at PyConES 2020. No other page has one: the lists of events, talks, and people, and the programme of an event, carry a link to the video and nothing else. What the frame does and does not do:

- When you open one of those pages and scroll near a video, your browser asks Google for the player. Google then sees your IP address and browser details, and that you opened that page on this site, as it would for an image loaded from its servers. This happens before you press play.
- Until you press play, Google states that this player stores nothing in your browser and sets no cookie.
- When you press play, you are watching a YouTube video inside this page, and Google's [privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) applies as it would on youtube.com.
- Under every player there is the plain link to the same video on youtube.com, so you can watch it there instead, and the page reads fine with the frame blocked.

### Cookies

This site sets no cookies of its own, and runs no analytics or tracking scripts. Its scripts, styles, fonts, and images are served from this site, with two exceptions: the membership pages load the PayPal button from paypalobjects.com, and the page of PyDay BCN 2018 loads the Eventbrite ticket button from eventbrite.es. Like any image host, those servers see your IP address and browser details.

### Hosting

The site is served by [GitHub Pages](https://pages.github.com). GitHub may log the IP address and request details of each visit for security and operation, under the [GitHub privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement). We do not see or receive those logs.

## Who else sees your data

Nobody buys or receives your data from us. The companies named above see it because we use their services: Google (forms, calendar, mailing lists, documents, and the YouTube player on the talk pages), PayPal (payments), Eventbrite (the ticket button on the page of PyDay BCN 2018), and GitHub (hosting and the source repository). All four are based in the USA. Each publishes the safeguards it applies to data from the EU, and we rely on those.

## Your rights

You can ask us to:

- see the data we hold about you (access);
- correct it (rectification);
- delete it (erasure), including a whole people page;
- limit how we use it (restriction), or object to a use (objection);
- give it to you in a usable format (portability);
- withdraw your consent, for a people page or anything else.

To do so, write to [pybcn@googlegroups.com](mailto:pybcn@googlegroups.com). Say what you want and which page or form it concerns. For a people page, write from the email address or one of the profiles linked on the page, so we know it is you. The law gives us one month to answer. We do it by hand, as volunteers, so a few days is normal.

If you think we handle your data wrongly, you can complain to the Spanish data protection authority, the [Agencia Española de Protección de Datos](https://www.aepd.es).
