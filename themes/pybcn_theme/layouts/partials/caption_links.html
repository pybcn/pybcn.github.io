{{- /* The caption of a gallery photo with its mentions, its hashtags, and its URLs as links. Argument: the caption, as the front matter holds it. Returns HTML, which the caller prints through safeHTML: {{ partial "caption_links.html" $item.caption | safeHTML }} A caption is the text of a post, written by somebody else. It reaches this partial as data and leaves it as markup, so the order of the two steps is the whole point: 1. htmlEscape first. Everything the caption holds becomes text: a < becomes < and cannot open a tag. AGENTS.md asks for no field that reaches a template through safeHTML, and this is the one exception the rule allows for, a field that is escaped before anything is added to it. bin/check-html-safety and bin/check-rendered are what hold the line, and both run on every pull request. 2. Then the three patterns below add the links, and nothing else can add markup, because the only < and > left in the string are the ones this partial wrote. What becomes a link: @pybcn https://x.com/pybcn #PyDayBCN https://x.com/hashtag/PyDayBCN https://... itself A mention and a hashtag go to x.com, and they carry no mark. Every other link to x.com on this site carries the X mark, and this is the one exception, on purpose. The mark is there to say where a link goes, and the figure already says it once: the credit under the photograph reads "Via @PyBCN" with the mark. A mark on each mention inside the quotation repeats that, and a caption holds up to several mentions, so the marks read as clutter in a sentence somebody wrote. Put them back by writing the i element of .icon_x into the two replacements below, after the escape, which is where markup this partial wrote belongs. A URL of the post keeps no mark either, because it may go anywhere. Three rules keep a link from eating what is next to it. A token has to start the caption, or follow a space, a "(", or a quotation mark. The quotation mark is written " or ' at this point, which is why the pattern names the two entities: a caption like "Keep your repo clean", by @hectorcanto_dev reaches here with every quote escaped. The rule is what stops the "#" of a URL fragment and the "@" of a URL with a user name from being read as a hashtag or a mention, and it stops any of the three patterns from matching inside the markup an earlier one wrote: a "(" or a space never follows the href= of a link. A URL may not end on a full stop, a comma, a colon, a semicolon, an exclamation mark, or a closing bracket, so the punctuation of the sentence stays outside the link. It may end on a "/", because that is part of the path: the 2022 caption carries https://pybcn.org/events/pyday_bcn/pyday_bcn_2022/ and the trailing slash belongs to it. A bracketed pair inside the URL is kept whole, which is how a link to a page with a bracket in its name survives while (https://pybcn.org) keeps its brackets outside the link. A URL may hold &, which is what a query string of two parameters looks like after step 1, and may hold nothing else that starts with an "&". So a URL followed by a quotation mark ends at the quotation mark, rather than swallowing the " that stands for it. The alt text of the photo is not this string. It is the caption itself, printed as text, because an alt attribute carries no markup: see the img of partials/photo_gallery.html. Measured over the 231 captions of the archive: 149 links on 97 captions, 5 of them URLs, with no link that holds the punctuation after it and none that holds a closing bracket of the sentence around it. Three captions store "&" where the post wrote "&", so they print "&" on the page. That is the stored text, and it printed that way before this partial existed: the fix belongs in the content, not here. */ -}} {{- $text := htmlEscape . -}} {{- /* The three patterns run in this order. A URL first, so that a "#" or an "@" inside one is already part of a link before the two patterns that read those two characters run. */ -}} {{- $text = replaceRE `(^|[\s(]|"|')(https?://(?:&|[-\w+@#/%?=~_|!:,.()\[\]])*(?:\([^\s()]*\)|[-\w+@#/%=~_|]))` `$1$2` $text -}} {{- $text = replaceRE `(^|[\s(]|"|')@(\w+)` `$1@$2` $text -}} {{- $text = replaceRE `(^|[\s(]|"|')#(\w+)` `$1#$2` $text -}} {{- return $text -}}
