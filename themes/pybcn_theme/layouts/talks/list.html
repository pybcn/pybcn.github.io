Six columns, in the order David Arcos sketched the row: the year, the month, the day, the event, the talk with its speakers, and the gallery card. It is the table layouts/_default/events_index.html draws for /events/, with the same structure, the same roles written out, the same rowspan group headers, the same caption and column headers, and the same stylesheet, listing_table.scss, so the two pages cannot drift. The header comment of that template says why a table, why the roles are written out, why the header row is hidden and not removed under the lg breakpoint, and why the day column prints the first day of an edition and not its range. None of that is repeated here; what follows is what this page adds. One more group level. /events/ has one row per day, so the year and the month are its groups. Here a row is a talk, and the 607 rows sit under 224 events: the event is a third group, between the month and the row, and it prints once, on the first row of its talks, spanning them with rowspan the way the month spans its rows. The event is a column of its own, as in the sketch, and a rowheader of scope row: a reader who moves down the Talk column of a screen reader hears the event of any talk from the header cell that covers it. The day spans the event too. The rows of one event share one day, so a day on every row would print "29" seventeen times under PyDay BCN (2025) and say nothing the event cell does not say. On /events/ the day is on every row because every row is a day. So the day cell takes the rowspan of its event, and holds the first day of an edition that ran more than one day, with the rule and the time element of events_index.html. The talk cell. The marker is the icon of its type from partials/event_types.html, named, so its label is the accessible name and the hover (see partials/event_type_icon.html): nothing else on the row says that a talk was a workshop. The title links to the page of the talk. After it, "by" and the speakers, each name a link through partials/person_page_link.html, which is the one place that decides where a speaker name goes: the page of that person, /people//, since David Arcos decided on a page per person. A talk that credits nobody, 87 of the 607, prints the title alone: a piece with no data behind it is left out rather than printed as unknown. Under lg the credit drops to its own line under the title, as the venue of /events/ does. The gallery is partials/count_card.html: a photo of the day that names the talk, its video, its other links. The counts come from partials/listing_counts.html, the one walk of the site that also feeds the legend above the table, so the card and the legend cannot disagree, and the card leaves out a count of zero. Headers and outline. The year is a th of scope rowgroup in its own tbody, with the year as its id, so /talks/#2019 links to a year from anywhere and the jump bar above the table reads the same ids. The month is a th of scope row, the event is a th of scope row. The table has its own outline, so the page has one heading, the h1 of the hero, which reads "Talks (607)" from partials/listing_total.html. Where the rows come from. Every talk page is added by content/talks/_content.gotmpl, and partials/talk_record.html joins each one back to the event that gave it. The year, the date, and the title of the row's event are the event's, read once per event through partialCached. The year is the year of that event, which every edition carries, and not the date of the page: one edition of the archive has no date, and a year is what the group needs. An event with no date would print an empty month and day, as on /events/; no event with talks is in that state today. The order. Years newest first, and inside a year the rows by the date of their event newest first, then the event title and its URL as the tiebreak for two events on one day, then the place of the talk in the programme of its event: a day is a sequence, and the first talk of the morning is not the one whose title starts with an A. The months and the events fall out of that order as runs of rows, and the render loop opens a group where the key of a row differs from the row before it.
